About the mission

The is based in San José, the capital of Costa Rica, and covers the western side of the country's Central Valley and areas farther to the west and northwest. It was created on July 1, 2015, when the original Costa Rica San José Mission was divided and the other half became the San José East Mission.

The Central Valley sits high in the middle of the country, ringed by mountains and volcanoes, and its mild weather is often described as spring all year. Like the rest of Costa Rica it has two seasons: a dry season early in the year and a green, rainy season that usually runs from about May to November. The lowlands toward the Pacific are much hotter.

Missionaries there teach in . Costa Ricans are known as “Ticos,” and you will hear “pura vida” everywhere, as a hello, a thank-you, and a way of saying life is good.

How a mission works

What is the MTC?

The Missionary Training Center, where new missionaries learn to teach and, if they need it, start on a new language. James began with home MTC training on August 12, 2026, then went to the Mexico MTC in Mexico City on August 27. From there he heads to Costa Rica, with his arrival in the field set for September 23.

What is a transfer?

Roughly every six weeks the mission president reassigns missionaries. James may stay put or be moved to a new town, and he may get a new companion either way. Transfer news usually shows up in the weekly email, and the Where He Is page gets updated when it does.

What is a companion?

Missionaries are always paired up. Companions live, study, and teach together for the length of a transfer or longer. You will see a lot of names come and go over his mission.

What is a P-day?

Preparation day — one day a week for laundry, groceries, sightseeing, and writing home. That is when the weekly email arrives, and when he is allowed to call or video chat with his family.
